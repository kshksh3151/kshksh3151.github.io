## Structure, Electronic Structure, and Charge Transport

Conjugated polymers are structurally heterogeneous materials whose electrical properties are governed not only by chemical composition, but also by molecular conformation, intermolecular packing, and structural disorder. These features shape the electronic states available for charge transport, making it difficult to understand macroscopic electrical behaviour from molecular structure alone. A central challenge is therefore to connect morphology across multiple length scales with electronic structure and the resulting charge-transport processes.

This connection is further complicated by the fact that structural disorder can influence different electronic processes in competing ways. A structural feature that facilitates one aspect of charge generation or localization may simultaneously hinder long-range transport. My research uses molecular simulation and multiscale modelling to identify the physical relationships linking polymer morphology, electronic structure, and charge transport, with the broader goal of developing more predictive approaches to conjugated polymer design.


## Electrochemical Doping and Dynamic Structural Evolution

Electrochemical doping is inherently dynamic because electronic charging, ion transport, solvation, and polymer structural reorganization occur simultaneously. As ions enter or leave a polymer, local electrostatic environments and molecular interactions change, while the polymer itself may swell or reorganize. These coupled processes can depend strongly on ion identity, polymer chemistry, and local morphology, making electrochemical doping difficult to describe using static structural models.

Understanding how these microscopic processes combine to produce macroscopic responses such as charging, swelling, and electrical transport is therefore an important challenge. My current research explores simulation strategies that describe electrochemical doping as an evolving molecular process, with particular interest in the coupling between ion motion, charge formation, and structural change. Ultimately, I aim to connect these molecular mechanisms with experimentally observable behaviour and materials design.
